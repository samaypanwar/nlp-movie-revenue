\section{Discussion}

The topic model captures recurring narrative themes. Its largest topic mixes
several settings and relationships, and the documentary topic includes
formulaic wording. These features help characterize the corpus, even though
the five topic weights make little difference to revenue prediction. Across
the tested models, the full controls leave limited incremental predictive
value for plot. Production company is particularly informative and may proxy
for project scale, distribution, or other unavailable inputs. The ablation
measures predictive contribution conditional on the model and the other
controls. It cannot separate those mechanisms.

The revenue analysis is conditional on recorded positive grosses, an
English-original-language overview, and a listed company. The labeled films
are a selected subset of the full corpus. Company coverage and label
availability can change over time. The source lacks per-film currency,
market scope, and receipt timing, so the CPI comparison addresses a
specific nominal USD assumption. The snapshots also leave open when an
overview or company record became available. The study is retrospective,
and the intervals describe uncertainty on the observed release years.
They do not account for shifts in future release cohorts or dependence
among related films.

A follow-up could compare the simple and full controls on exactly the same
films to identify when the earlier embedding improvement disappears. Two
further sensitivity checks would allow genre-specific release-year trends
and add budget on the budget-observed subset. Better revenue provenance and
dated pre-release metadata would make the forecasting interpretation more
direct.
