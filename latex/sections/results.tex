\section{Results}

\subsection{Coverage and narrative topics}

Genre coverage increases sharply in the later decades, while CPI-adjusted
medians move differently across genres (Figure~\ref{fig:coverage}). Films can
belong to multiple genres. The amount of labeled data is therefore sensitive
to both release period and the choice of genre.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{genre_by_decade.png}
  \caption{Labeled film counts and median CPI-adjusted revenue for the five most
  common genres among pre-2014 labeled films. Medians require 30 films.
  The 2010s are partially observed. The adjustment assumes comparable nominal USD.}
  \label{fig:coverage}
\end{figure}

NMF recovers recognizable themes around family, romance, and war
(Table~\ref{tab:topics}). The largest topic includes family and coming-of-age
language and accounts for about 48\% of overviews. The film and documentary
topic also captures formulaic descriptions, as illustrated by its representative
film. All five topics retain the same ten highest-weight words in both
perturbed fits. This checks local stability under the tested initializations.

\subsection{Incremental prediction from plot}

On 2,660 evaluation films, the full controls model has pooled log MAE of
1.666. TF--IDF increases MAE by 0.093 and the frozen embedding increases it by
0.025. NMF changes MAE by about 0.002 (Table~\ref{tab:plot}). In 2014--2016,
embedding MAE rises from 1.678 to 1.684, with a paired interval that spans
zero. Under the CPI sensitivity, the pooled embedding change is $+0.020$ and
the final-window change rounds to zero.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Pooled incremental performance from adding plot to full controls.
  Positive changes indicate higher error. Intervals are paired.}
  \label{tab:plot}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lrrl@{}}
    \toprule
    Added representation & Log MAE & $\Delta$ MAE & 95\% interval \\
    \midrule
    TF--IDF & 1.759 & $+0.093$ & $[0.067, 0.119]$ \\
    NMF weights & 1.668 & $+0.002$ & $[\approx 0, 0.004]$ \\
    Frozen sentence embedding & 1.691 & $+0.025$ & $[0.005, 0.045]$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Figure~\ref{fig:paired} shows the embedding comparison by release window and
major genre. The clearest increase in error by window occurs in 2009--2011
($+0.061$, interval $[0.021, 0.101]$). In 2014--2016, drama and thriller show
small improvements, but their intervals cross zero. The intervals also cross zero for both
latest-window company-support groups. Genre-by-company intersections are
thin. The largest contains 18 films in 2014--2016 and falls to 17 after splitting
by collection membership.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{paired_plot_errors.png}
  \caption{Paired change in log MAE after adding the frozen plot embedding.
  Bars show pointwise 95\% intervals. Negative values favor adding plot.
  Genre groups overlap.}
  \label{fig:paired}
\end{figure}

The pooled result still allows material changes for individual films. In the
final window, adding the embedding reduces absolute log error for
\emph{The Martian} from 2.749 to 0.841 and increases it for \emph{The BFG}
from 0.312 to 2.334. These examples show why a few successful predictions
provide limited evidence of a systematic gain.

\subsection{Contribution of control blocks}

Removing production company increases pooled log MAE by 0.166, the largest
of the control ablations (Table~\ref{tab:controls}). In 2014--2016 the
corresponding increase is 0.232, with an interval of $[0.147, 0.318]$.
The smaller pooled contributions of runtime, genre, release timing, and
franchise membership are measured with all other controls present.
